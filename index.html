How the Freelance Rate Calculator Works

Freelance pricing isn't just "salary divided by hours." Here's why each input matters.

Why taxes matter

As a freelancer, no employer withholds or matches your taxes. Your hourly rate has to generate enough pre-tax revenue that, after taxes are paid, you still take home your target income.

Why business expenses matter

Software subscriptions, equipment, insurance, and other costs of running a business come out of your revenue before you see any take-home pay. These need to be priced into your rate, not treated as an afterthought.

Why freelancers can't bill every working hour

Time spent on admin, invoicing, marketing, proposals, and client calls is real work, but it usually isn't billable. Most freelancers can only bill somewhere between 50% and 80% of their total working hours — this calculator uses your utilization estimate to adjust for that.

Why vacation time affects your rate

Freelancers don't get paid time off. If you want to take vacation without losing income, the weeks you're not working need to be covered by the weeks you are — which raises the rate you charge during your working weeks.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should a freelancer charge per hour?

It depends on your income goal, taxes, business expenses, and how many hours you can actually bill. Instead of guessing, use a calculation that starts with the annual income you want to take home and works backward to an hourly rate that covers taxes, expenses, and unpaid time.

How do I calculate my freelance hourly rate?

Start with your desired take-home income, add taxes and business expenses to find your required revenue, then divide that by the number of hours you can realistically bill in a year. This calculator does that math for you automatically.

What percentage of my time should be billable?

Most freelancers can only bill 50–80% of their working hours once you account for admin, marketing, client communication, and finding new work. A common starting estimate is 70%, but track your own time to find your real number.

Should freelancers charge for vacation time?

Freelancers don't get paid time off, so vacation weeks need to be built into your rate in advance. This calculator subtracts your vacation weeks from the year, which increases your hourly rate to make up for the weeks you won't be billing.

What is the difference between hourly rate and take-home income?

Take-home income is what you keep after taxes and business expenses. Your hourly rate has to generate enough revenue to cover both of those things and still leave you with your target take-home income.
